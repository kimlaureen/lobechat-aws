# Q1 — Use case design

## Context

A 75-person AWS cloud consultancy manages infrastructure for 20+ enterprise clients simultaneously across e-commerce, fintech, and media verticals. Each client engagement runs on a dedicated AWS account with a shared services layer — VPC peering, centralised CloudWatch, Terraform state in S3 — maintained by the firm's platform team.

Today engineers maintain six to eight browser tabs per active engagement: AWS Console, CloudWatch, CloudTrail, an SSH terminal, internal Notion runbooks, and AWS documentation. Context-switching between these is the dominant productivity tax. A routine billing investigation — identifying why a client's AWS bill spiked — takes a senior engineer 2–4 hours of manual data retrieval, correlation, and write-up.

The firm cannot use ChatGPT or Copilot for client-facing work: their MSAs prohibit client infrastructure data from reaching third-party APIs. This blocks AI assistance at precisely the moments where it would be most valuable — incident response and client deliverables — and forces engineers to strip out all client-specific detail before pasting anything into a commercial model, making the output generic and requiring significant rework.

The firm deploys this LobeChat stack on a single EC2 instance in their own AWS account. All LLM inference for data-sensitive steps runs locally on vLLM (Gemma 4 E4B with native function calling). Client data never leaves the EC2 host.

## Personas

**María — Solutions Architect / Engagement Lead**

María owns three client relationships. Her week splits between designing architectures, producing client deliverables (diagrams, cost reports, architecture reviews), and answering questions from junior engineers. She spends 20–30% of her time producing documentation that is stale within weeks. She uses ChatGPT for drafting but strips out all client-specific details before pasting, which makes the output generic and requires substantial rework.

Her core frustration: the institutional knowledge she builds about each client lives in her head or in Notion pages nobody reads. When she takes leave, her clients effectively lose their primary context-holder.

**Dani — SRE / Cloud Operations Engineer**

Dani handles incident response, routine health checks, and deployment verification across all active client accounts. During incidents, Dani is simultaneously SSH'd into servers, watching CloudWatch, reading Notion runbooks, and writing Slack updates. Every context switch is time the client's service is degraded.

Dani's core frustration: finding the right runbook at 2am under pressure, when Notion search returns three outdated versions of the same document.

**Pau — Junior Consultant**

Pau joined six months ago and is still building mental models of client infrastructure. They spend the first weeks on each new account asking María questions she has answered six times before for other engineers. They are reluctant to run commands in production-adjacent environments they do not fully understand, which means every action requires approval and senior engineer time.

## Journey — Billing investigation

A client calls: their AWS bill spiked 40% this month. María takes the call and opens LobeChat.

**Turn 1 — Identify the spike (2 min)**

*"Client X AWS bill spiked 40% this month. What's driving it?"*

`aws-resources-operations` fires — boto3 `get_cost_and_usage` against the client's linked account, grouped by service, monthly granularity. The model returns a ranked cost delta: EC2 up 58%, NAT Gateway data transfer up 31%, everything else flat. Two suspects identified in one turn. The local Gemma 4 model handles this step; raw cost data never leaves the EC2 instance.

**Turn 2 — Find the culprit resources (3 min)**

*"Which EC2 instances launched in the last 30 days? Show CPU utilization and estimated monthly cost."*

`aws-resources-operations` fires twice: `describe_instances` filtered by launch time, then `get_metric_statistics` from CloudWatch for CPU. Returns three `r6i.4xlarge` instances launched 18 days ago, tagged `env=staging, project=data-migration`, running at 2–4% CPU. Estimated cost: $1,800 over 18 days.

**Turn 3 — Trace the launch to an IAM principal (2 min)**

*"Who launched those three instances?"*

`aws-resources-operations` calls CloudTrail `lookup_events` scoped to the three instance IDs. Returns: launched by `arn:aws:iam::ACCOUNT:role/ci-pipeline-data-migration`. No JSON scanning, no manual filter-building.

**Turn 4 — Retrieve internal runbook context (2 min)**

*"Do we have any internal documentation about a data migration project for Client X?"*

`qdrant-mcp` fires against the internal RAG corpus — runbooks, post-mortems, and architecture decision records indexed from past engagements. Returns a chunk from a runbook written six weeks ago: *"teardown after migration sign-off — no automated lifecycle policy defined."* Surfaced inline, attributed to the source file.

María now knows the instances are intentional but the teardown was missed — without messaging anyone on Slack or knowing in advance which document to look for.

**Turn 5 — Confirm instances are idle via SSH (2 min)**

*"SSH into those three instances and confirm there's no active workload."*

`ssh-exec` runs `ps aux`, `netstat -an`, and `uptime` on each host. All three return: no meaningful processes, no active connections, load average under 0.1. Idle confirmed.

**Turn 6 — Generate the architecture diagram (2 min)**

*"Generate a diagram showing the three instances, their VPC, subnet, and NAT Gateway. Flag the idle resources."*

`infrastructure-diagrams` generates and executes a Python `diagrams` script using Graphviz (installed in the custom MCPHub image). Produces a PNG. `minio-mcp` uploads it to the MinIO bucket and returns a public URL. The diagram appears inline in the chat — three EC2 nodes marked in red, connected to the NAT Gateway generating the transfer charges. María copies the URL directly into the client slide deck.

**Turn 7 — Draft the client summary (1 min)**

*"Write a client-facing Slack message summarising findings and next steps."*

This step routes to a larger frontier model via OpenRouter — the summary contains no raw infrastructure data, only synthesised findings, so the data sovereignty constraint does not apply. The model drafts: cost breakdown, IAM principal, runbook reference, idle confirmation, and three recommendations (terminate instances, add budget alert, implement lifecycle tagging policy). María edits two sentences and sends.

**Total elapsed: 14 minutes. One chat window. Zero tab switching.**

María stores the findings back into the corpus: *"Store this investigation in the knowledge base."* `qdrant-mcp` fires in write mode. The next engineer who encounters a similar cost spike on any client retrieves this context without asking anyone.

**Time saved: approximately 196 minutes per investigation — a 94% reduction.** The manual version (Cost Explorer export, EC2 console drill-down, CloudTrail JSON scan, Notion search, SSH terminal, client write-up) takes 2.5–3 hours for a senior engineer. At a blended SA rate of $200/hour, that is ~$650 recovered per incident. Across a 10-SA firm running three investigations per month each, the stack recovers ~$78,000/year in billable capacity previously consumed by manual data retrieval.

## Why this stack vs ChatGPT/Copilot

**The tool calls cannot be replicated in ChatGPT Enterprise.**

`aws-resources-operations` calls boto3 directly against the client's AWS account using the EC2 instance's IAM role — no stored credentials, no keys to rotate. `ssh-exec` connects over the private network to the client's servers with a whitelisted command set. ChatGPT Enterprise has no mechanism to reach either. The engineer returns to acting as the data retrieval layer themselves — manually exporting Cost Explorer CSVs, scanning CloudTrail JSON, and pasting SSH output — which is the 3.5-hour manual workflow this stack eliminates. ChatGPT Enterprise is a better text editor for the same broken process, not a replacement for it.

**Uploading the RAG corpus to OpenAI violates client MSAs.**

Turn 4 depended on a Qdrant corpus stored on the EC2 instance — internal runbooks containing client-specific architecture details, incident histories, and operational procedures. Replicating this step in ChatGPT requires uploading that corpus to OpenAI's servers. Most enterprise consulting MSAs explicitly prohibit client operational data from reaching third-party APIs. The prohibition is contractual, not merely a privacy preference; OpenAI's "no training on Enterprise data" guarantee does not resolve it. The RAG step either does not exist in ChatGPT, or it creates a legal exposure the firm cannot accept.

**The model routing decision does not exist in ChatGPT.**

Turns 1–5 route to local Gemma 4: raw AWS cost data, CloudTrail IAM principals, EC2 configurations, and SSH process lists stay on the EC2 instance. Turn 7 routes to a frontier model via OpenRouter: synthesised, non-sensitive content where output quality matters. ChatGPT Enterprise is a single endpoint — every tool result goes to OpenAI regardless of sensitivity. The firm either accepts that client infrastructure telemetry leaves their control on every investigation, or they do not use the tool for real client work. Either outcome breaks the workflow. The self-hosted stack is the only architecture that allows AI assistance on sensitive data while keeping that data sovereign.
